How does variation reveal organization? · Fractal physiology

Bruce J. West

A way of asking whether biological variability reflects coordination across scales—and whether fractional and nonlinear models can explain patterns that ordinary averages leave invisible.

One contribution at the tableWest brings mathematical models of scale, memory, coupling, and crucial events. His work is especially useful when it is kept clear whether a paper reports data, reanalyzes data, simulates a model, or proposes a mechanism.

01 · Foundation

Why is a healthy rhythm not perfectly regular?

Heartbeats, strides, and other biological events fluctuate. West's work treats some of this variability as organized across time rather than as independent error around a mean.

Key shiftAsk about relations among time scales: how quickly influence decays, whether patterns repeat statistically across scale, and how constraints alter that organization.

02 · Developing

What changes when a person follows an external rhythm?

Free walking and metronome-paced walking can have similar average stride intervals but different temporal organization. The gait work asks how voluntary and environmental constraints reshape persistence, variability, and multifractal structure.

03 · Advanced

When does a fractional model explain more than a fitted slope?

Fractional and nonlinear models can compactly describe memory across scales. Explanatory force depends on distinguishing a model's assumptions from fitted evidence, comparing plausible alternatives, and testing predictions outside the data used to motivate it.

Source

West and Scafetta propose a stochastic central-pattern-generator coupled to a forced nonlinear oscillator to reproduce gait timing under free and metronomic conditions.

Open

Reproducing selected statistical patterns does not establish that the proposed neural and motor-control components are the biological generator.

04 · Data-and-model record

A nonlinear dynamical model of human gait

West & Scafetta (2003) · Physical Review E, 67, 051917

The paper reanalyzed an existing gait dataset and compared its scaling patterns with simulations from a stochastic central-pattern-generator model coupled to a forced Van der Pol oscillator.

Sample
10 healthy adults; mean age 21.7 years, range 18–29
Design
Slow, normal, and fast walking, freely and with a metronome
Data
About 60 minutes per free-walking series and 30 minutes per paced series
Analysis
Wavelet-based local Hölder-exponent distributions plus model simulation

What the record shows

  • Unconstrained gait showed persistent, near-1/f temporal structure in the analyzed data.
  • Metronome pacing generally reduced long-range persistence and increased between-person diversity.
  • The proposed model qualitatively reproduced several reported changes across speed and pacing conditions.

Read the evidence carefully

  • The human dataset was small and reused from prior work rather than collected as a new confirmatory test.
  • Several model parameters were chosen to resemble observed patterns rather than estimated and validated out of sample.
  • Histogram resemblance is not a formal comparison against competing generative models.
  • Interpretations of model parameters as neural stress or conscious constraint remain hypotheses.
